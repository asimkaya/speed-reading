## Q1. Which products exist, what method/structure do they use, and what price points (one-time vs subscription) exist and what do they include?

### Takeaway
The market splits into (a) cheap/free RSVP & drill tools (Spreeder CE, AccelaReader, ReadSpeeder free; Reading Trainer ~$3 / €2.99), (b) mid-priced self-paced video courses with lifetime access ($67–$199 one-time: Iris Reading, Spreeder VIP lifetime, Udemy), (c) app subscriptions (~$5–10/month: Acceleread, Elevate), and (d) premium guru/membership funnels ($199–$399+/year or $97–$99/month: Jim Kwik / Kwik Success, Mindvalley). Mid-2026 trend: guru players are moving from one-off courses to memberships (Kwik closed standalone course sales on 30 June 2026; Mindvalley sells only memberships), and apps are adding "AI" (Kwik AI tutor, Spreeder AI content/summaries).

## Q3. What evidence or criticism exists regarding their claims?

### Takeaway
The scientific consensus (Rayner et al., 2016, Psychological Science in the Public Interest) is that speed-reading claims of high speed with maintained comprehension are overstated. RSVP specifically hurts comprehension on longer texts, and the reliable way to read faster is to become a more skilled reader/language user. Marketing across the category is aggressive: "3–10× faster", "300% faster in 21 days", "307 books a year", "10X faster with AI". The FTC's 2016 $2M Lumosity settlement is the regulatory precedent for unsupported cognitive-training claims.

### Cited Findings
- Rayner, Schotter, Masson, Potter & Treiman (2016), "So Much to Read, So Little Time: How Do We Read, and Can Speed Reading Help?", Psychological Science in the Public Interest — [SAGE journals](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1529100615623267), [APS summary](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/publications/speed_reading.html)
  - Claims of super-speed reading with good comprehension are "overstated and not in line with what we know" about visual/cognitive language processing; doubts on using peripheral vision to take in a whole line/page — [ScienceDaily, Jan 2016](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2016/01/160114163035.htm)
  - Under RSVP, comprehension suffers and worsens the longer the text, since readers can't go back (hold a sentence, not a page) — [ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2016/01/160114163035.htm), [READINGSOFT: Why speed reading apps do not work](https://readingsoft.com/speed-reading-apps/)
  - A speed–accuracy trade-off exists; the route to faster reading with comprehension is practice and language skill such as vocabulary — [APS](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/publications/speed_reading.html)
- Stanislas Dehaene: claims of reading up to 1,000 wpm "must be viewed with skepticism" — [Wikipedia: Speed reading](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speed_reading)
- Jim Kwik criticism: described as teaching "pseudoscientific" material centred on "misunderstandings about subvocalization"; the Kwik Reading course reportedly tells students to reduce subvocalization, then later to ask questions while reading (internal contradiction) — [Magnetic Memory Method (competitor Anthony Metivier)](https://www.magneticmemorymethod.com/jim-kwik-speed-reading/). Note: this critic sells competing memory courses, so there is a conflict of interest. A competitor built an alternative program partly "as an antidote to the pseudoscience" in Mindvalley Super Reading — [e-student Super Reading review](https://e-student.org/super-reading-review/)
- Claim inventory (aggressiveness):
  - "Read 3 to 10 Times Faster" (7 Speed Reading) — [software.informer](https://7-speed-reading.software.informer.com/)
  - "Read Up to 3× Faster" (Acceleread) — [accelereadapp.com](https://accelereadapp.com/)
  - "Read 300% Faster in 21 Days" (Kwik Reading) — [start.kwikbrain.com/reading](https://start.kwikbrain.com/reading)
  - "at least double your reading and learning speed in just 21 days" (Mindvalley Super Reading) — [mindvalley.com/superreading](https://www.mindvalley.com/superreading)
  - "How To Read 307 Books In 2026" (Udemy) — [Udemy](https://www.udemy.com/course/speed-reading-mastery/); "Read & Learn 10X Faster with ChatGPT & AI" — [Udemy](https://www.udemy.com/course/speed-reading-read-learn-10x-faster-with-chatgpt-ai/); "understand 300 books per year" — [Udemy](https://www.udemy.com/course/brain-friendly-reading-understand-300-books-per-year/)
  - "+143% in 10 days" (Reading Trainer) — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id416814366)
  - "Double Your Reading Speed" (Iris free class) — [irisreading.com](https://irisreading.com/free-speed-reading-class/)
  - Comparatively modest: ReadSpeeder "Comprehend Faster, To Read Faster" — [readspeeder.com](http://www.readspeeder.com/)
- Regulatory precedent: FTC, Jan 2016 — Lumos Labs paid $2M over unfounded claims that Lumosity improves work/school performance and delays cognitive decline, and over undisclosed incentivised testimonials — [FTC press release](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2016/01/lumosity-pay-2-million-settle-ftc-deceptive-advertising-charges-its-brain-training-program), [Science/AAAS](https://www.science.org/content/article/brain-game-maker-fined-2-million-lumosity-false-advertising)

### Inferences
- Honest positioning, such as "comfortable reading at natural rhythm, not 1,000 wpm magic", cites Rayner and treats punctuation-aware pauses as a comprehension safeguard. It is both a real differentiator and a regulatory/ethical hedge, and it matches the project's own RESEARCH.md stance.
- The multiplicative claims ("3×", "10×", "300%") are the category norm, so a moderate claim may look weaker in ads; evidence-based framing with measured comprehension can compensate.

### Gaps
- No 2024–2026 peer-reviewed study evaluating specific commercial products (Spreeder, Iris, Kwik) found.

- Reddit/Trustpilot complaint patterns could not be read in full (Trustpilot for Iris has only 2 reviews; Reddit not retrieved beyond a snippet saying some Kwik teachings "are not possible according to our current understanding of the human body" — [via search on Jim Kwik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Kwik) — attribution uncertain).

### Inferences
- Turkish demand is exam-driven (LGS/YKS) and served by in-person/1-on-1 tutoring at high TL prices. A good free/cheap Turkish RSVP app with honest comprehension metrics has no direct global competitor in Turkish, and competes locally with course providers and Okumatik-style tools.
- Global RSVP tools technically accept Turkish text, but none are known to handle Turkish-specific tokenization (agglutinative long words, Turkish abbreviations), which is a product edge.

- No evidence found of any Turkish localisation of Iris, Kwik, Mindvalley's speed reading programs or Spreeder; absence is unconfirmed rather than proven.
- French/Spanish/Italian equivalents were not researched due to tool-call budget.
