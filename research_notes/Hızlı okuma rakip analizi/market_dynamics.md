## 1. History and fate of notable RSVP players

### Takeaway
Spritz (2014) was the hype peak: viral demo, ~$3.5M seed, OEM deals with Samsung, a free SDK with 25,000+ developer signups, but no settled business model; it still exists as a small "active" company with an app, but never became a platform. The durable survivors are free/cheap utilities (Spreed -> SwiftRead) and a buried feature inside Kindle (Word Runner), not standalone RSVP businesses.

### Cited Findings
- Spritz (Boston-based; in stealth since 2011) closed a $3.54M seed round in March 2014; investors included Denis O'Brien via telco Digicel — [TechCrunch, 2014-03-10](https://techcrunch.com/2014/03/10/spritz-seed/)
- Crunchbase lists total funding of $4.38M over 3 rounds and operating status "Active" (Spritz Technology) — [Crunchbase](https://www.crunchbase.com/organization/spritz); Tracxn also keeps a 2026 profile — [Tracxn](https://tracxn.com/d/companies/spritz/__EsR2pV8tUzcwnNt88c4-glLqxDkUUDaUBXs2X7mwoN4)
- Founders named as Maik Mauer, Jamie Locke and Frank Waldman (CEO); founding year given as 2012; later described as based in Salt Lake City, Utah (sources conflict on HQ: Boston vs Salt Lake City) — [Crunchbase](https://www.crunchbase.com/organization/spritz); [TechCrunch video with Frank Waldman](https://techcrunch.com/video/frank-waldman-of-spritz/)
- Spritz streamed one word at a time at 100–1,000 wpm; demo video went viral and "more than 10 million people" reportedly asked when they could use it (company claim) — [Daily Dot](https://www.dailydot.com/news/spritz-speed-reader/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2014/03/10/spritz-seed/)
- Samsung's Gear 2 smartwatch and Galaxy S5 (announced at MWC Feb 2014) were to ship with Spritz inside their email apps — [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/amp/wbna54565304); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2014/03/10/spritz-seed/)
- Spritz partnered with e-book subscription service Oyster, offering Covey's "The 7 Habits of Highly Effective People" free in Spritz format — [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/amp/wbna55002731)
- April 2014: Spritz shipped a free SDK (web first, then Android/iOS); 25,000+ developers had signed up; the business model "was not cemented yet"—it wanted users first, with an ambition of 1 billion readers, and planned to monetize via licensing to device makers, websites and app makers — [TechCrunch, 2014-04-01](https://techcrunch.com/2014/04/01/spritz-launches-sdk-to-bring-its-speed-reading-technology-to-websites-and-apps/)
- Spritz later offered a bookmarklet, browser extensions and PDF-reader integration; claims licensing interest from content providers, OEMs, ed-software, wearables and e-book firms; a patent filed 2016-08-01 was granted 2019-06-25 — [nouvellenoire.ch (Spritz reseller/partner page)](https://nouvellenoire.ch/type_solutions/spritz-for-speed-reading/)
- A "Spritz App" exists on the US App Store (app id 1486315785) — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/spritz-app/id1486315785)
- Early scrutiny: EdSurge covered Spritz as "attracting funding and scrutiny" in March 2014 — [EdSurge, 2014-03-17](https://www.edsurge.com/news/2014-03-17-speed-reading-spritz-app-attracts-funding-and-scrutiny); Virginia Heffernan's critique "Real Reading, Fake Reading" — [Medium/The Message](https://medium.com/message/real-reading-fake-reading-724eab421390)
- OpenSpritz (2014, Rich Jones / Gun.io): free, crude JS bookmarklet cloning Spritz, grammar-aware pauses; created because there was "no official Spritz reading app yet"; many GitHub forks and an Android ePub/Google Glass port followed — [Gun.io](https://gun.io/news/2014/08/openspritz-a-free-speed-reading-bookmarklet/); [GitHub brandly/OpenSpritz](https://github.com/brandly/OpenSpritz)

- Adjacent "reading hack" player Bionic Reading (Swiss, not RSVP; bolds word beginnings) monetizes mainly via licensing (font, IP, API, volume licenses); customers include HarperCollins and Malaysia's Lumi News; won German Innovation Award 2023 Gold. A 2024 peer-reviewed paper is titled "No, Bionic Reading does not work" — [Bionic Reading suppliers](https://bionic-reading.com/br-for-suppliers/); [BR pricing](https://bionic-reading.com/br-pricing/); [Acta Psychologica / ScienceDirect 2024](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0001691824001811). Its consumer app is listed under app studio MWM — [MWM](https://mwm.ai/apps/bionic-reading/1644658030)

### Inferences
- Spritz's pattern: huge top-of-funnel curiosity, free SDK to maximize reach, OEM embedding (Samsung) — but embedding as a feature in someone else's email app gives no user relationship and no revenue lever. Crunchbase's total of only $4.38M after the 2014 hype suggests no significant follow-on round.
- The App Store id range (~1.48 billion) implies the current Spritz App was published around late 2019, i.e., a pivot toward a direct-to-consumer app years after the licensing strategy.
- Commoditization was instant: within weeks of Spritz's reveal, free open-source clones (OpenSpritz, Squirt, Spreed) replicated the core UX. RSVP itself is not defensible IP; the "ORP/red letter" alignment is the only patented angle.
- Amazon shipping Word Runner (with dynamic pacing) on Kindle and then barely promoting it signals that even the platform owner with full content access did not see RSVP as a major engagement driver.

## 3. User behavior, retention and scientific evidence

### Takeaway
Peer-reviewed evidence is consistently skeptical: speed-comprehension trade-off is real (Rayner et al. 2016), and RSVP/Spritz specifically lowers literal comprehension and reduces blinking, increasing visual fatigue (Benedetto et al. 2015). The most promising positive evidence is niche: small-screen reading (smartwatch/glasses) and a 2025 study showing RSVP improved comprehension for young adults with ADHD while hurting neurotypical controls.

### Cited Findings
- Rayner, Schotter, Masson, Potter & Treiman (2016), Psychological Science in the Public Interest 17(1):4–34: there is a speed–accuracy trade-off; claims of super-speed reading with good comprehension are "overstated"; modest gains possible through practice/exposure to words (i.e., being a more skilled reader) — [SAGE](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1529100615623267); [PubMed 26769745](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26769745/); [APS press release](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/observer/speed-reading-promises-are-too-good-to-be-true-scientists-find-2); [ScienceDaily 2016-01-14](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2016/01/160114163035.htm)
- Benedetto et al. (2015), Computers in Human Behavior, n=60 (mean age 28): compared normal reading vs Spritz; suppression of parafoveal processing and of regressions reduced literal comprehension; a significant reduction in eye blinks with Spritz may increase visual fatigue — contradicting Spritz's claims — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0747563214007663); [PDF](https://www.tsw.it/wp-content/uploads/Rapid-serial-visual-presentation-in-reading-The-case-of-Spritz-1.pdf)
- "Rapid Serial Visual Presentation: degradation of inferential reading comprehension as a function of speed" (c. 2018) — inferential comprehension degrades as RSVP speed increases — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/327269635_Rapid_Serial_Visual_Presentation_degradation_of_inferential_reading_comprehension_as_a_function_of_speed)
- ADHD (2025): "Reading without eye movements: Improving reading comprehension in young adults with ADHD", Journal of the International Neuropsychological Society, published 2025-11-24, University of Toronto Scarborough. Undergraduates with/without ADHD read short paragraphs in FULL text, PACED (one word at its usual location), and RSVP. ADHD participants did best in RSVP and benefited by almost 13% relative to neurotypical controls, who showed comprehension difficulties in RSVP — [Cambridge Core](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-the-international-neuropsychological-society/article/reading-without-eye-movements-improving-reading-comprehension-in-young-adults-with-attentiondeficithyperactivity-disorder-adhd/33851CEA7C1AC6193D088D4D5551ED3C); [PubMed 41277242](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41277242/); earlier version [ResearchGate 2022](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/364348418_Reading_without_eye_movements_Improving_reading_comprehension_in_young_adults_With_ADHD)
- ~45% of people with ADHD have coexisting learning disabilities, dyslexia most common (context for accessibility segment) — search snippet from ADHD/dyslexia literature, e.g. [CHADD](https://chadd.org/adhd-news/adhd-news-educators/reading-comprehension-and-executive-function/) (attribution approximate)
- AI speed reading tool for students with ADHD, dyslexia and short attention span (arXiv 2023, prototype, not an efficacy RCT) — [arXiv 2307.14544](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.14544)
- Small screens: RSVP reading on a smartwatch (Max Planck / Dingler et al., 2016) — with eye-tracking-based implicit support, reading speed raised to 150% of normal while comprehension stayed nearly stable — [MPG PDF](https://pure.mpg.de/rest/items/item_2567078_1/component/file_2567077/content); [ResearchGate "RSVP on the go"](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/310820843_RSVP_on_the_go_implicit_reading_support_on_smart_watches_through_eye_tracking)
- Smart glasses (CHI 2018): comparing text positions with RSVP vs line-by-line scrolling, RSVP yielded higher comprehension while sitting — [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3173574.3173619)
- NLP-improved RSVP: "Thoth" varies timing using NLP (2019) — [arXiv 1908.01699](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1908.01699); "Improving RSVP Reading by Considering Text Complexity" (2020) — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/346056335_Improving_RSVP_Reading_By_Considering_Text_Complexity)
- Scientific American framed RSVP as "Speed-Reading Reborn for Smartphones, Smartwatches" — [Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/speed-reading-reborn-for-smartphones-smartwatches/)
- Blog claim: "meta-analysis of RSVP studies found comprehension drops of 20–40%... above 500 wpm comprehension often below 50% for complex texts" — [readlite.in](https://readlite.in/concepts/rsvp-reading/) / [SpeedTextLab](https://speedtextlab.com/blog/what-is-rsvp-reading) (LOW reliability: marketing blogs, meta-analysis not identified — do not cite as fact)
- User critique of Kindle Word Runner as "worthless" (practical usability complaints) — [Good e-Reader](https://goodereader.com/blog/electronic-readers/amazon-word-runner-is-worthless)

### Inferences
- Plausible use pattern (consistent with the evidence, not directly measured): RSVP is used for short, low-stakes text (articles, emails, notifications, re-reading familiar material), not for dense or literary books where regressions matter.
- The comprehension penalty and fatigue argue for features our POC already leans toward: moderate default WPM, punctuation/complex-word pauses (like Kindle's Dynamic Pacing and Thoth), easy pause with sentence context (regression substitute), and short sessions.
- Positioning RSVP as "focus mode" / "reading without losing your place" (ADHD-friendly) is better supported by evidence than "read 3x faster".

### Gaps
- No public app-analytics data (session length, D1/D30 retention) for any RSVP app was found. Retention claims must be treated as inferences.
- No study found on RSVP in Turkish (an agglutinative language with long words — potentially important for timing).
- No robust dyslexia-specific RSVP efficacy study found in this session.

## 4. Monetization models for reading/learning apps

### Takeaway
Education apps convert poorly relative to other categories, and annual plans dominate; median Education prices are ~$8/month and ~$44/year (RevenueCat 2025). Successful adjacent reading products use subscriptions (Speechify $139/yr, Readwise Reader $9.99/mo, SwiftRead ~$50/yr) or B2B licensing (Bionic Reading, Spritz's original plan).

## Key Question A: Why haven't RSVP apps become mainstream, and what segments sustain them?

### Takeaway
RSVP failed to go mainstream because the core promise (much faster with equal comprehension) is scientifically contested, the tech was instantly cloned for free, platform owners (Samsung, Amazon) treated it as a minor feature, and it fits only short, low-stakes texts. Sustaining segments are self-improvement/productivity users reading web articles (SwiftRead), small-screen contexts, and potentially ADHD/attention-challenged readers.

### Cited Findings
- Comprehension trade-off and overstated claims — [Rayner et al. 2016](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1529100615623267)
- Lower literal comprehension, fewer blinks/fatigue with Spritz — [Benedetto et al. 2015](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0747563214007663)
- Free clones within weeks of Spritz launch — [Gun.io OpenSpritz](https://gun.io/news/2014/08/openspritz-a-free-speed-reading-bookmarklet/); [Spreed](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/spreed-speed-reader/dajafpbgegmeplpfcigmpofamomphkem)
- Spritz had no settled business model when launching SDK — [TechCrunch 2014](https://techcrunch.com/2014/04/01/spritz-launches-sdk-to-bring-its-speed-reading-technology-to-websites-and-apps/)
- ADHD benefit, neurotypical penalty — [JINS 2025](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-the-international-neuropsychological-society/article/reading-without-eye-movements-improving-reading-comprehension-in-young-adults-with-attentiondeficithyperactivity-disorder-adhd/33851CEA7C1AC6193D088D4D5551ED3C)
- SwiftRead continues to sell Pro subscriptions to a web-article audience — [Chrome Web Store](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/swiftread-read-faster-lea/ipikiaejjblmdopojhpejjmbedhlibno)

### Inferences
- Platform access is a structural barrier: most long-form reading happens inside Kindle/Apple Books/closed apps where third-party RSVP cannot reach content; RSVP apps are left with copy-paste, web pages and DRM-free files.
- Novelty decay is likely (viral curiosity in 2014 did not translate into sustained companies), but direct retention data is absent.

### Gaps
- No direct retention/churn figures for RSVP apps.

## Key Question B: Realistic pricing and distribution for a small independent team

### Takeaway
Keep costs near zero, go web-first (PWA, no store fee), and treat revenue as modest: a freemium tier with a low annual plan (benchmarks: Education median ~$44/yr globally; SwiftRead ~$50/yr) adjusted down heavily for Turkey, plus exploring B2B (schools/test-prep) as the more promising revenue path.

### Cited Findings
- Education median $8.13/mo, $43.94/yr; weak Education conversion; App Store converts better than Google Play — [RevenueCat 2025](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2025)
- Trials raise Education LTV (+50.4%); 12-mo LTV $45.10 — [Adapty](https://adapty.io/blog/education-app-subscription-benchmarks/)
- Indie RSVP app ~$440/mo net — [TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/rsvp-reader)
- Licensing/B2B precedent (Bionic Reading volume/API licenses) — [Bionic Reading](https://bionic-reading.com/br-for-suppliers/)

### Inferences
- In Turkey, Google Play (Android) dominates and converts worse than iOS per RevenueCat's platform gap, further lowering consumer subscription expectations.
- A browser extension (Spreed/SwiftRead playbook) is the proven low-cost distribution channel for RSVP.

### Gaps
- No Turkish-market data on willingness to pay, Android/iOS share, or school procurement.

## Key Question C: Differentiators that could matter

### Takeaway
The strongest defensible differentiators are those that address RSVP's known weaknesses and platform gaps: native-language (Turkish) tuning of timing/ORP, built-in comprehension checks, ADHD/focus positioning backed by 2025 evidence, and B2B education packaging; "faster reading" alone is commoditized.

### Cited Findings
- Text-complexity-aware / NLP pacing improves RSVP — [Improving RSVP by text complexity](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/346056335_Improving_RSVP_Reading_By_Considering_Text_Complexity); [Thoth](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1908.01699); Kindle's Dynamic Pacing — [The Digital Reader](https://the-digital-reader.com/word-runner-is-amazons-solution-to-the-speed-reading-problem-and-it-just-may-work/)
- Inferential comprehension degrades with speed (justifies comprehension checks) — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/327269635_Rapid_Serial_Visual_Presentation_degradation_of_inferential_reading_comprehension_as_a_function_of_speed)
- ADHD RSVP comprehension benefit — [JINS 2025](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-the-international-neuropsychological-society/article/reading-without-eye-movements-improving-reading-comprehension-in-young-adults-with-attentiondeficithyperactivity-disorder-adhd/33851CEA7C1AC6193D088D4D5551ED3C)
- Evidence-free claims get publicly debunked (Bionic Reading) — [ScienceDirect 2024](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0001691824001811)

### Inferences
- Honest, evidence-aligned marketing ("comfortable, focused reading; measured comprehension") could itself differentiate against competitors' "read 3x faster" claims and reduce reputational risk.
- Turkish public-domain literature and school/test-prep reading passages give a local content moat that global apps lack.

### Gaps
- No evidence found on whether any Turkish-language RSVP competitors exist or how they perform.
