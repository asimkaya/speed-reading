Method note: research done 2026-09-27. Most official sites (bionic-reading.com, speechify.com, beelinereader.com, elevenreader.io, apps.apple.com, sciencedirect.com, pmc/pubmed, phys.org) were blocked by the network proxy for direct fetching, so many figures below come from search-result snippets of the official pages or from third-party pricing trackers. ## 1. Product profiles: what each does, claims, evidence, platforms, pricing, users, Turkish support

### Takeaway
The adjacent market splits into (a) visual text-formatting aids (Bionic Reading, BeeLine) that are cheap (about $2-10/mo) but have weak or negative independent evidence for speed; (b) text-to-speech "listen faster" apps (Speechify, ElevenReader, NaturalReader, Voice Dream) that are the biggest and most expensive ($60-$160/yr) and mostly support Turkish; (c) book-summary apps (Blinkist, Headway, Shortform, $80-$200/yr) that replace reading instead of speeding it, with little or no Turkish content; and (d) free built-in platform features (Apple Accessibility Reader, Kindle, Android Reading Mode, Readwise Ghostreader) that set a "free baseline".

  - Readwise informal online test (2022) of 2,074 readers: Bionic readers were on average 2.6 wpm *slower* (<1%, i.e. no effect) — [Readwise blog](https://blog.readwise.io/bionic-reading-results/)
  - Early skeptical commentary: The Conversation (2022) "Can Bionic Reading make you a speed reader? Not so fast" — [The Conversation](https://theconversation.com/can-bionic-reading-make-you-a-speed-reader-not-so-fast-183905); independent self-test by Andy Kong — [andykong.org](https://andykong.org/blog/bionicreadingtest/)

## 2. Which capture the "save time reading" budget, and at what price points?

### Takeaway
TTS apps (Speechify ~$139/yr, 50M+ users claimed) and summary apps (Blinkist ~$80-100/yr, Headway ~$90/yr, 50M+ downloads) capture most of the "save time" spend; formatting aids capture small amounts ($2-10/mo); OS vendors now give decent reading/listening tools away free.

### Cited Findings
- Speechify $139/yr or $29/mo; 50M+ users claimed — [Costbench](https://costbench.com/software/ai-voice-tools/speechify/); [Speechify blog](https://speechify.com/blog/is-speechify-free-to-use/)

### Inferences
- A plausible price anchor for a consumer "read faster" subscription is ~$2-5/mo (formatting-aid tier) up to ~$8-12/mo (TTS/summaries). For Turkey, local-currency pricing would be much lower (app stores price by region); summary/TTS apps' USD anchors are not directly applicable.
- The "save time" budget increasingly goes to *listening* and *AI summaries*, not visual tricks; an RSVP app must argue it keeps the full text (unlike summaries) and is faster than listening (TTS at 1x ~150-180 wpm; 2x ≈ 300-360 wpm) — wpm figures for speech rate are general knowledge, not sourced here.

## 3. What can an RSVP app learn from their positioning, integrations and monetization?

### Takeaway
Winners integrate everywhere text lives (browser extension, share sheet, PDF/EPUB/web import), sell a clear free tier with a paid "unlimited/premium" tier, run education/institutional licensing, and use accessibility (ADHD/dyslexia) as positioning — but the science-checking of claims has hurt Bionic Reading's credibility.

### Cited Findings
- Speechify is on iOS, Android, Chrome, web, Mac, won an Apple Design Award for inclusivity — [TextToLab](https://texttolab.com/blog/speechify-review)
- Bionic Reading monetizes via freemium apps + API licensing + volume licences (CHF 69-89/licence/yr) — [BR Pricing](https://bionic-reading.com/br-pricing/); [BR for Suppliers](https://bionic-reading.com/br-for-suppliers/)
- BeeLine sells class/school/district licences and offers free teacher access; positions as "helping readers finish what they start" — [BeeLine education](https://www.beelinereader.com/education/register); [beelinereader.com](https://www.beelinereader.com/)
- NaturalReader separates personal and commercial licences — [NaturalReader commercial](https://help.naturalreaders.com/en/articles/9363601-plans-pricing-commercial-version)
- Headway uses gamification (streaks, flashcards, spaced repetition) and short billing cycles (4-week plans) — [Headway pricing](https://makeheadway.com/blog/how-much-does-the-headway-app-cost/)
- ElevenReader uses student offers (1 year free) — [Resourify](https://resourify.com/resources/eleven-reader)
- Voice Dream's switch from one-time purchase to subscription triggered a public backlash from its accessibility community — [Perkins](https://www.perkins.org/resource/voice-dream-reader-subscription-controversy/); [Mosen.org](https://mosen.org/voicedream/)
- Bionic Reading's efficacy claims were publicly refuted by peer-reviewed studies ("No, Bionic Reading does not work", 2024; UCL 2026) — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0001691824001811); [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12565662/)

### Inferences
- For a Turkish RSVP app: priority integrations = share-sheet/"open in" on mobile, a browser extension, paste/URL import; later PDF/EPUB (out of POC scope per PROJECT.md).
- Honest positioning (comprehension-aware, "natural rhythm", no miracle-wpm claims) differentiates against Bionic-style hype; measure and show comprehension/time saved in the finish summary.
- Institutional channel: Turkish schools/dershane/university libraries (class/school licences à la BeeLine) and accessibility NGOs.
- Combining RSVP with optional TTS or AI summary would compete head-on with the bundles users already pay for; avoid one-time-to-subscription switches later (Voice Dream lesson) — decide the model early.

### Gaps
- No data found on conversion rates (free→paid) for any of these apps.

## 4. Where are they weak (Turkish support, comprehension, eye strain)?

### Takeaway
Turkish is well served only by TTS (Speechify, ElevenReader); summary apps and Kindle Word Wise essentially lack Turkish content, and Bionic Reading only "officially" supports English. Visual aids show no speed benefit; summaries sacrifice the full text; TTS is bounded by comfortable listening speed (~2x).

### Cited Findings
- Headway languages exclude Turkish — [Headway pricing blog/search snippet](https://makeheadway.com/blog/how-much-does-the-headway-app-cost/)
- Blinkist content mainly English/German; no Turkish found — [Befreed](https://www.befreed.ai/blog/blinkist-pricing-2026)
- Kindle Word Wise only for select English books — [Amazon help](https://www.amazon.com/gp/help/customer/display.html?nodeId=TABlJ4ot69emTO8jJG)
- Bionic Reading officially English — [BR FAQ](https://bionic-reading.com/faq/)
- Speechify and ElevenReader support Turkish — [Speechify Turkish](https://speechify.com/text-to-speech-online/turkish/); [ElevenLabs help](https://help.elevenlabs.io/hc/en-us/articles/49649705435665-What-languages-does-ElevenReader-support)
- Bionic Reading: no speed/comprehension gain (2024, 2026 studies; Readwise 2,074-reader test) — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0001691824001811); [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12565662/); [Readwise](https://blog.readwise.io/bionic-reading-results/)
- BeeLine: helps only with difficult layouts, slows easy layouts, children found it less convenient — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2451958822000318)
- Speeded listening: minimal comprehension cost from 1x to 1.5x-2x for lecture videos, but performance declines beyond 2x — [ResearchGate: "Learning in double time"](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/356194270_Learning_in_double_time_The_effect_of_lecture_video_speed_on_immediate_and_delayed_comprehension); [PMC: lecture playback speeds](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10354881/)
- General skepticism about speed-reading app claims (The Conversation, 2024) — [The Conversation](https://theconversation.com/some-speed-reading-apps-make-big-claims-but-how-fast-can-we-actually-read-287497)

### Inferences
- Gap for a Turkish-first RSVP app: no competitor offers Turkish-tuned visual reading (Turkish agglutinative long words matter for ORP and word-length timing; Bionic's bolding isn't tuned for Turkish).
- TTS apps' 4.5x claims exceed where studies show comprehension holds (~2x); an RSVP app can credibly target 300-500 wpm with comprehension checks.
- RSVP shares a weakness with TTS: no regressions (re-reading) — the app's pause-context feature is a mitigation worth emphasizing.

- No studies found on eye strain for Bionic/BeeLine/RSVP specifically in 2024-2026.

## 5. Accessibility angle: RSVP/visual tools marketed for ADHD or dyslexia — what evidence says

### Takeaway
Evidence is thin and mixed: one recent study suggests RSVP improved comprehension for young adults with ADHD (while hurting neurotypical controls); BeeLine's dyslexia/ADD claims are founder-reported; Bionic Reading shows no benefit in skilled readers; dyslexia research flags RSVP-related attentional-blink issues.

### Cited Findings
- RSVP & ADHD: study "Reading without eye movements: Improving reading comprehension in young adults with ADHD" — ADHD participants did better in RSVP than in eye-movement reading conditions, benefiting ~13% relative to neurotypical controls, who had comprehension difficulties with RSVP — [PubMed 41277242](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41277242/)
- Dyslexia & RSVP: literature on attentional blink in dyslexia is mixed (e.g., "No evidence for a prolonged attentional blink in developmental dyslexia") — [PubMed 20800831](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20800831/)
- BeeLine's claim (users with dyslexia/ADD +50% speed) comes from founders' testing, not independent peer review — [Wikipedia: BeeLine Reader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BeeLine_Reader)
- Bionic Reading: no speed/comprehension benefit for skilled adult readers (2026, n=90) — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12565662/)
- Platform accessibility tools (Apple Accessibility Reader, Android Reading Mode, Kindle Assistive Reader) are free and target low vision/dyslexia — [Apple Support](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/read-listen-text-apps-accessibility-reader-iph406a46ab8/ios); [AlternativeTo: Reading Mode](https://alternativeto.net/software/reading-mode/about); [Amazon Assistive Reader](https://www.amazon.com/gp/help/customer/display.html?nodeId=TqLHvK6eo6O7DJVQoZ)

### Inferences
- An RSVP app could cautiously position for ADHD focus ("fewer eye movements, fewer distractions") citing the ADHD study, but should not claim benefits for dyslexia; the neurotypical-comprehension penalty in that same study reinforces the need for natural pauses and pause-context.

### Gaps
- Could not read full texts (proxy block) of the ADHD RSVP study for sample size, year, WPM used; verify before citing publicly.
- No 2024-2026 RCTs on RSVP for dyslexia found.
